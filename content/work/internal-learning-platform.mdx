## Context

An organization of about 900 people needed one place for its training: a
programme catalogue, sessions, who asked to attend, who approved, who came, who
is certified, replacing an older system whose data was migrated in. I started the platform alone, took it to production in about
three months, and still own every layer of it. A second developer joined on
features along the way.

## What I owned

- **Product:** the domain rules, written down as numbered invariants, each tied
  to a test, and the decisions behind them recorded as ADRs.
- **Architecture:** .NET 10 Clean Architecture with CQRS on PostgreSQL, a
  separate worker for scheduled jobs, and a Next.js front end behind a BFF, so
  tokens never reach the browser. Single sign-on with Entra ID, user sync from
  the HR system, an email queue with retries.
- **DevOps:** the GitLab server and its runner, the CI/CD pipeline, Docker
  Compose builds behind nginx with TLS, separate development, test and
  production environments, tagged releases with generated notes.
- **Operations:** a logging strategy with structured logs kept in the database
  and archived on a schedule; a tiered backup strategy with a stated recovery
  point, and restores rehearsed rather than assumed; a daily read-only health
  check of production.

## How a task moves

Every change follows the same governed cycle. AI does most of the typing; the
cycle decides what counts as done, and there is a review between every step.

The same setup gives the agent its tools through MCP servers for GitLab, the
browser and the design files, and keeps the rules in the repository, so any
agent, or any new developer, starts from the same contract.

## Proof before go-live

Before the platform went live, it was load- and stress-tested up to 300
concurrent users with no server errors and a 95th percentile under 400 ms, and
scanned with SonarQube, OWASP ZAP and a dependency audit.

## Outcome

The platform went from first commit to production in about three months and
now runs the organization's training. More than 2,000 automated tests guard it,
and production incidents end as a rule, a test and a decision record, so the
same failure cannot come back unnoticed.
