## Context

Every quote starts from the catalog. It decides what can be sold, with which
options, at what price and under which terms. In telecom it is also the part
that changes most often: new speeds, new bundles, new discounts. If those live
in code, every commercial change waits for a release.

## How it is modeled

We followed TM Forum's product and service catalog model (TMF620, TMF633,
TMF634), so the catalog speaks the same language as the rest of the industry.

<LayerStack title="From what is sold to what delivers it">
  <Layer label="Offering" detail="what is sold" note="Bundles, validity dates, versions, sales channels." />
  <Layer label="Product" detail="what it is" note="Characteristics, their allowed values and how they depend on each other." />
  <Layer label="Service & resource" detail="what delivers it" note="The services and resources behind the product." />
  <Layer label="Price" detail="what it costs" note="Prices, taxes, tiered discounts, penalties and contract terms." />
</LayerStack>

Nothing goes on sale half-finished. Every catalog item moves through the same
lifecycle, and the allowed moves between states are kept in one map. Changing
the flow means changing that map, not the services that use it.

## What it gives the business

- Options, prices, discounts and validity dates are data, maintained from the
  catalog screens.
- The lifecycle keeps unfinished items from going live.
- The TM Forum model makes the catalog easier to connect to other systems,
  because the terms are shared.

A new kind of product can still need new logic when the order is split into
its parts. The catalog covers what changes most often, and that is where the
time was going.
