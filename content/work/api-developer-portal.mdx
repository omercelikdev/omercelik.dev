## Context

A bank wanted to open its APIs to business partners through a developer
portal: partners sign up, try the APIs in a sandbox, get certified, go live and
are charged for use. Behind it sat an existing API gateway and the core banking
system.

I worked through the bank's requirements, about 165 of them, mapped each to a
part of the solution, proposed the approach and the target architecture, and
reviewed the bank's own high-level design against them.

## What I proposed

- **One approval component.** Partner sign-up, product access, go-live and
  changes all need approval by someone other than the requester, sometimes in
  several stages. One shared maker-checker component instead of an approval
  flow per screen.
- **Provisioning that survives a failing gateway.** Changes to the gateway go
  through an outbox with retries, and a nightly job reconciles what the portal
  thinks is live with what the gateway actually has.
- **Protect the core.** Calls to the core banking system go through a circuit
  breaker and a cache, so a slow core doesn't take the portal down.
- **Scheduled jobs that run once.** A single-run scheduler with idempotent jobs,
  so a job never runs twice when the portal runs on more than one node.

## What the design review found

The review was about questions the design left open, the ones that turn into
incidents later:

- The session scope of the one-time-password step-up was not defined.
- The sandbox topology was not decided.
- Where fee collection ends and the bank's systems begin was not documented.
- The non-functional requirements had no numbers.
- Reports would run against the live database.

Each finding came with a recommendation, so the review could be acted on, not
just read.
